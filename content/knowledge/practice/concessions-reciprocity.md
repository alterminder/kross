# Conditional Concessions and Reciprocity

## What it is for

A concession is something you give up or add to get closer to agreement. Many negotiation courses teach the same rules of thumb: trade concessions, do not give them away, and watch what each one signals. This note sets out that practice advice, separates it from CCHN doctrine, and shows how CCHN frames the same moment.

## The method

**Practice advice (not CCHN):**
- **Make concessions conditional.** Phrase them as "If you…, then we…", with the condition first. That makes a concession part of an exchange rather than a gift.
- **Avoid unilateral concessions.** A concession given for nothing can signal that more will follow and invite further demands.
- **Watch size and pace.** Concessions that shrink and slow down signal that you are near your limit. Large, fast ones signal the opposite.
- **Only trade what you are authorised to give.** A concession outside your mandate is not yours to make.

**The reciprocity norm (Cialdini, *Influence*):**
- People feel obliged to repay what they receive, including concessions.
- Cialdini describes the "rejection-then-retreat" pattern. A larger request is refused, and a smaller one that follows feels like a concession that deserves one in return.
- It works on you too. A counterpart's favour, hospitality or "flexibility" can create a felt debt that pushes you to give more than you should.

## At the table

- **Tie terms together and put them in sequence.** "If you can confirm the route in writing by Wednesday, we can schedule the first convoy for Thursday morning."
- **Offer an alternative that stays inside your limits.** "Paying the guards in food rations is not something I can agree to. Their families can receive rations in the general distribution if they register like everyone else. Would that help?"
- **Name the pause point instead of conceding further.** "That goes beyond what I can agree today. I need to consult my hierarchy, and I will come back to you."

## What to avoid

- **Conceding under emotional pressure or to end an outburst.** CCHN says this rewards the use of emotion (FM p. 188).
- **Agreeing to a term between your bottom line and your red line without instructions.** CCHN says this requires further instructions from the mandator (FM p. 291).
- **Trading something the counterpart wants for personal gain**, such as money, goods or favours for an individual commander (see below).
- **Matching another organisation's concessions** because the counterpart points to them. CCHN says the negotiator "should not buckle" (FM p. 287).
- **Leaving traded terms vague.** Vague terms become a liability at implementation (FM p. 159).

## How this relates to CCHN

**Where it agrees**
- **The transaction is an exchange of commitments.** In CCHN, the transactional stage is when the Common Shared Space becomes "a definite series of reciprocal commitments" (FM p. 152). Every transaction ends with commitments to act for the other side's benefit (FM p. 154).
- **Conditional, sequenced terms.** Tool 7's third criterion says well-set terms recognise the reciprocal and interdependent character of commitments, including "the order in which these tasks should proceed and the conditional nature of particular tasks" (FM p. 172). This is the closest CCHN comes to "if you…, then we…".

**Where CCHN differs**
- **Relationship before trade.** CCHN treats humanitarian negotiation as relational: agreements derive from the relationship (FM p. 110).
  - *Transactional drift.* If a commander seeks "a personal advantage … in the form of money or goods", the negotiation quickly becomes transactional, driven by his interest at the expense of the relationship and trust (FM p. 110). Reciprocity that pays an individual is the drift CCHN warns about.
  - *Institutional red lines.* Some trades are ruled out entirely, for example using food rations as payment or giving direct assistance to armed personnel in the FWB example (FM p. 290).
- **Quick wins are allowed.** CCHN's tier system (Tool 14, FM p. 305) starts with Tier 1: low-cost, high-benefit issues that build the relationship and set a positive tone. Tool 6 asks you to create shared value before claiming benefits (FM p. 162). An early goodwill step is not the same as conceding under pressure. A reasonable reading (practice, not CCHN text) is to apply "no concession without return" to Tier 2 issues, where both sides bear some cost.
- **Bottom line, not a concession curve.** Instead of shrinking your concessions, CCHN asks you to pause at your bottom line:
  - Before it, you are in open dialogue.
  - Between it and the red line, you need instructions.
  - Beyond the red line, the option is off the table (Tools 13–14, FM pp. 285–295).
  - Citing your bottom line lets you pause "without breaking the relationship" (FM p. 288).

**Use instead:** Tool 7 for the terms; Tool 14 for tiers and scenarios; Tool 13 for shared benefit; Tool 17 for red lines.

## Try instead

* *Instead of:* "OK, we'll add your district to the convoy." (given for nothing, under pressure)
* *Try:* "If we can settle the checkpoint arrangements for the first convoy this week, we can plan your district into the second one, under the same procedures."

* *Instead of:* "Fine, we'll pay the guards in food."
* *Try:* "Paying guards with rations is not something I can agree to. Their families can receive rations in the general distribution if they register like everyone else. Would that help?"

## How KROSS uses it

- **Your limits.** Each entry in `issues` has an ideal outcome, a bottom line, a red line and a tier. `trainee_brief.mandate` says what you `can_decide` and what you `must_refer`.
- **Committing too far.** `unauthorized_commitment` marks a commitment outside your mandate. It raises `mandate_breach` (a must-refer item agreed without referring) or `red_line_crossed` (a term beyond a red line).
- **Clear terms.** `clarifies_operational_detail` rewards pinning down who, what, when and where, and marks items in `implementation_checklist`.
- **Alternatives.** `offers_options` rewards proposing alternatives instead of conceding the point under discussion.
- **Dials.** `rigidity` drops as options appear. `trust` drops if you change position unexpectedly (`unexpected_position_change`).
